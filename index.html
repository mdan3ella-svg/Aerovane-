SIX ASSEMBLIES / FOUR ROTORSVERTASCAN / 2026

01 — THE DESIGN INTENT

Design dossier ↓

Four stations.
One fluid form.

AEROVANE V4 is a two-seat flying-platform study: a faceted central capsule suspended between four independent propulsion pods.

The pointed nose, broad shoulders and exposed triangulated links express a lightweight mechanical architecture. A continuous glossy black canopy forms a quiet visual centre. Pearl body panels, dark struts and crisp fan surfaces make the assembly legible from every angle.

Its name joins the open-air ambition of the concept with the directional control of a vane. The four-station monogram and descending central keel echo the vehicle’s layout.

02 — SIX ASSEMBLY GROUPS

Separate the form.
Understand the motion.

01 / FRAMEWORK

The central capsule

The faceted body, black canopy and mechanical struts share the main framework assembly. Independent material zones let you colour the shell, glazing and links.

02–05 / FOUR PODS

Stationary fairings

Each pod is partitioned from the supplied merged mesh. The pod shells remain fixed while the fan surfaces turn. Suspension-like links stay with the framework.

06 / FAN ASSEMBLY

Four axial rotors

Fan surfaces are rebuilt within four outboard apertures, with individual X-axis pivots and counter-rotation. This provides clean animation without rotating fairings or struts.

DISPLAY DYNAMICS

A measured rise

Gradual fan spool, damped vertical settling and subtle vibration accompany hover and yaw controls. Optional synthesized fan, airflow, beep and hydraulic sounds follow the interaction.

03 — CONCEPT CADD REVIEW

Proportions made explicit.

PROVISIONAL ENVELOPE

4.80 × 3.20 × 1.31 m

Length is a design assumption. Width and height follow the mesh proportions. The source contains no verified dimensions. The broad track supports the four-pod composition.

VISIBLE LAYOUT

2 seats / 4 pods

Two seats are visible in the cabin reference. Outboard fan openings suggest approximately 0.66 m rotor diameter at the assumed scale. Cabin access appears to use a lifting canopy; hinge geometry is unverified.

PRESENTATION LIMIT

Hover is illustrative

The visible fan axes are lateral. Vertical lift would require tilting ducts, downward-flow routing or another lift architecture that the supplied geometry does not establish.

04 — TECHNOLOGY & FEASIBILITY

A concept with an honest
engineering question.

AVAILABLE TECHNOLOGY

Distributed electric drive

A candidate architecture uses independently controlled electric motors, inverters, a high-voltage battery, inertial sensing and closed-loop flight control. Composite shells and machined metal attachment nodes suit the visual intent.

SIZING CASE / NOT A SPECIFICATION

850 kg / 4 × 200 kW

Illustrative gross mass and peak motor budget for a first trade study. A 60–80 kWh battery is a candidate energy budget, not a verified package or flight endurance claim. Rotor containment, cooling and redundancy need development.

COMPACT ROTOR PENALTY

≈418 kW ideal hover

Four 0.66 m disks provide only about 1.36 m² of area. At 850 kg and sea-level density, ideal momentum theory gives approximately 418 kW. With assumed 0.65 figure of merit and 0.92 drive efficiency, the estimate rises to about 700 kW before installation losses.

The hover estimate assumes all four disks produce vertical thrust, unlike the displayed lateral-axis geometry. A 60 kWh battery with 80% usable energy would support roughly four minutes at 700 kW before reserves and other loads. This flags a major sizing challenge, not a claimed operating capability.
